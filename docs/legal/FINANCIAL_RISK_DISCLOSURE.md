# Financial Risk Disclosure

Investing involves risk. You can lose part or all of the money you invest.

* **Market risk:** prices of stocks, ETFs and bonds go up and down, sometimes sharply and for long periods.
* **Credit risk:** bond issuers can fail to pay coupons or principal.
* **Interest-rate risk:** when interest rates rise, prices of existing bonds usually fall.
* **Inflation risk:** inflation reduces the purchasing power of savings and fixed payments.
* **Currency risk:** if an investment is in a different currency than yours, exchange-rate changes
  affect your result — this depends on the currencies of the underlying assets, not only on the trading
  currency.
* **Liquidity risk:** some instruments cannot be sold quickly without a price discount.
* **Concentration risk:** putting a large share into one company, sector or country increases risk.
* **Costs:** fees, spreads, currency conversion and taxes reduce returns.
* **Past performance does not guarantee future results.** Historical statistics, scenarios and models
  shown in InvestiQ describe the past and model assumptions only.
* **Investor protection schemes** (e.g. the Czech Garanční fond obchodníků s cennými papíry, or deposit
  insurance for cash at banks) may cover losses from the **failure of an intermediary** under certain
  conditions and limits. They **never** protect against market losses.
* Money you need for housing, food, education, healthcare, debt repayment or an emergency fund is
  generally not suitable for investing in volatile assets.
